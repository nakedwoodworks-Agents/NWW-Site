Wood is the traditional gift for a 5th wedding anniversary, so the simplest good answer is something made of wood that the two of you will use and keep. We make engraved maple and walnut cutting boards, serving boards and coasters for exactly this, each made to order in Pryor, Oklahoma, with your names and wedding date on it.

## Why wood marks five years

The traditional anniversary list starts light and gets sturdier as the years add up: paper for the first year, cotton for the second, leather for the third, fruit or flowers for the fourth. Wood arrives at year five, and the usual explanation is that a marriage at five years has put down roots. You do not need the symbolism to justify the gift, though. A wood piece holds up to use, gets better looking with a little oil, and sits on a counter where people see it every day.

Anniversary lists vary by source. The common modern list puts wood at year six instead, which means a wood gift covers both lists if you are unsure which one your spouse follows. Our [anniversary gifts by year guide](/guides/anniversary-gifts-by-year/) lays out both lists side by side.

## Maple or walnut

Both are solid hardwoods, and every board we make is one solid slab. Maple is light and creamy, and the engraving shows up as a crisp dark brown. Walnut is a deep brown, a little softer, and the engraving reads quieter against it. If the kitchen is bright and white, maple tends to stand out; if it has dark cabinets or butcher block, walnut often fits in better. Our [maple vs. walnut guide](/guides/maple-vs-walnut-cutting-board/) goes into the practical differences.

## Picking a size

Our boards come in four sizes:

- 8x12 in, 0.75 in thick: a good everyday size and easy to wrap.
- 10x14 in, 0.75 in thick: roomier for serving, still light enough to handle.
- 12x16 in, 1.5 in thick: a heavy, substantial board that stays put on the counter.
- 16x24 in, walnut only, 1.5 in thick: a large serving and display piece.

For a 5th anniversary, the 10x14 is a common middle ground. The 12x16 is the one to pick if you want the gift to feel like a real piece of furniture for the kitchen.

## What to engrave

Keep it short. First names, the shared last name or initial, and the wedding date are enough for most boards. If you want to add a line, a short phrase or "5 years" under the date works better than a long quote, which gets small and hard to read on a board. Our guide on [what to engrave on a cutting board](/guides/what-to-engrave-on-a-cutting-board/) covers name order, hyphenated names and date formats.

Before we engrave anything, we send a free digital proof so you can check spelling and layout. Look closely at the date. It is the most common thing people want to change.
