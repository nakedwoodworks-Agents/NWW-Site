Choose maple if you want the engraving to stand out sharply and the board to feel bright and clean; choose walnut if you want a darker, richer board that shows fewer knife marks and stains. We cut every engraved board from one solid slab of either wood and laser engrave it to order in Pryor, Oklahoma, so the choice comes down to looks, use and budget.

## How the engraving looks on each

This is the difference people notice first.

**Maple** is pale, close to cream, with a tight, even grain. When the laser engraves it, the wood darkens to a warm brown. The contrast is high, so thin script, small dates and detailed monograms read clearly from across a room. Designs with lots of fine lines, like the [Circle Monogram Family Name Cutting Board](/p/circle-monogram-family-name-cutting-board/), look crisp on maple.

**Walnut** is a deep chocolate brown with more figure in the grain. The engraving still shows, but the contrast is lower. The result is quieter and more understated, which many people prefer for a board that stays out on the counter. Bold lettering and simple shapes hold up best on walnut. Our [Corner Logo Cutting Board](/p/corner-logo-cutting-board/) is a good example: a small logo in one corner of a walnut board looks refined rather than loud.

If your design has a lot of small text, lean maple. If it is a bold name or a simple mark, either wood works.

## How they hold up in the kitchen

Both are hardwoods that are commonly used for cutting boards, and both last for years with basic care.

- **Hardness.** Hard maple is the harder of the two. It resists dents well and has long been a standard choice for butcher blocks.
- **Knife marks.** Walnut is a little softer, which is easier on knife edges. Its dark color hides scratches, so it keeps looking tidy with daily use.
- **Stains.** Maple's light surface shows stains from things like berries, beets and red wine more readily. Walnut hides most of them.
- **Grain.** Maple's grain is fine and closed. Walnut's grain is a little more open and has more visible pattern.

Neither wood needs special treatment. Hand wash, dry right away and oil when the surface looks pale or dry. Our [care guide](/guides/how-to-care-for-an-engraved-cutting-board/) has the full routine.

## Matching the kitchen

A few rules of thumb:

- White or light cabinets, marble or quartz counters: maple blends in; walnut becomes a contrast piece. Both work, they just do different jobs.
- Dark cabinets, butcher block or warm wood tones: walnut usually fits in; maple can look very bright.
- Farmhouse or rustic: either, though maple's contrast suits script-heavy designs like the [Established Family Name Wedding Board](/p/established-family-name-wedding-board/).

If the board is a gift and you do not know the kitchen, maple is the safer bet for readability and walnut is the safer bet for hiding wear.

## Sizes and thickness

Our boards come in the same sizes in both woods, with one exception:

- 8x12 in, 0.75 in thick, maple or walnut
- 10x14 in, 0.75 in thick, maple or walnut
- 12x16 in, 1.5 in thick, maple or walnut
- 16x24 in, 1.5 in thick, walnut only

The 1.5 in boards are noticeably heavier and sit solidly on the counter. The 0.75 in boards are easier to lift and store.
